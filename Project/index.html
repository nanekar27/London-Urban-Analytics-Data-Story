The Story of London's 33 Boroughs

London is not one city — it is 33 distinct boroughs, each shaped by a unique combination of crime, property prices, and education. This dashboard explores how these three forces interact across the capital.

Where does crime concentrate?

Westminster, Newham, and Southwark consistently top the crime charts, driven by high footfall and population density. Violence and anti-social behaviour dominate across all boroughs, but inner London sees more theft while outer boroughs report more vehicle crime and burglary.

Conclusion: Three Londons

Our analysis of 1.2 million crime records, 73,000 property sales, and 3,195 schools reveals that London is not one city but three distinct realities operating within the same boundary.

Inner Affluent

Westminster, Kensington, Camden

Highest property prices in the country. Despite the wealth, crime rates are high driven by density, tourism, and nightlife rather than deprivation. Schools are well-resourced with low FSM rates.

Inner Deprived

Newham, Tower Hamlets, Hackney

The triple challenge: high crime, lower property values, and schools under pressure with FSM rates above 30%. Disadvantage concentrates across all three dimensions, creating a cycle that is difficult to break.

Outer Suburban

Bromley, Sutton, Havering, Bexley

Lowest crime rates, most school capacity per resident, and moderate property prices. These boroughs represent a different model of urban living, safer and better schooled but further from economic centres.

Key Finding

Crime, housing, and education are not independent variables. They form an interconnected system where deprivation in one domain reinforces disadvantage in the others. Effective policy requires coordinated intervention across all three, not siloed approaches that address each in isolation.
